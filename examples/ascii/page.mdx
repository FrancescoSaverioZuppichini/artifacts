<Section id="intro" title="Why draw with characters" open>
A terminal has no pixels, only a grid of letters. Pick letters by how much ink they put on the cell and the grid
becomes a grey-scale screen: `.` is almost black, `@` is almost white. This page renders a few classic toys that
way, draws each frame into an image with a monospace font, and hands the frames to ffmpeg. Everything comes from
one script, `make.py`, so rerunning it rebuilds every video, still and the generated section at the end.

<Badge>★ start with the donut</Badge> <Badge>6 s each</Badge> <Badge>24 fps</Badge> <Badge>H.264</Badge>
</Section>

<Section id="donut" date="Tue 17 Nov 2026 · 15:00" title="How the donut works" badges={["★", "torus", "two rotations"]}>
**The shape.** Take a circle of radius 1, sitting 2 units away from the y axis, and sweep it all the way round that
axis: that is a torus. Every point on it has two angles, θ around the small circle and φ around the axis.

**The motion.** Each frame turns the whole torus by two angles, A around x and B around z, so it tumbles instead of
spinning flat. A frame is just a different A and B.

**The picture.** Each point is pushed back 8 units, divided by its depth (things far away get small), and dropped
into a 100 × 42 grid of cells. Cells are twice as tall as wide, so y is halved. A z-buffer keeps only the nearest
point per cell.

<Code title="The core of donut() in make.py" text={`x = cx * (cb * cp + sa * sb * sp) - cy * ca * sb
y = cx * (sb * cp - sa * cb * sp) + cy * ca * cb
ooz = 1 / (k2 + ca * cx * sp + cy * sa)          # one over depth
col = int(COLS / 2 + k1 * ooz * x)
row = int(ROWS / 2 - k1 * ooz * y / 2)           # cells are twice as tall
light = cp * ct * sb - ca * ct * sp - sa * st + cb * (ca * st - ct * sa * sp)
if light > 0 and ooz > depth[row][col]:          # facing us, and nearest so far
    depth[row][col] = ooz
    grid[row][col] = shade(light / 1.5)`} />

<Section id="shading" date="Tue 17 Nov 2026 · 15:30" title="Shading with 12 characters" badges={["❓ your call", "luminance ramp"]}>
The light value is the surface normal dotted with a light behind the viewer, from 0 (edge on) to about 1.4 (facing
it). Divided by 1.5 it lands in 0 to 1, and that picks one of twelve characters, from thin to heavy:
`.,-~:;=!*#$@`. The ramp is the whole trick: swap it for `" .:-=+*#%@"` and the donut looks softer, swap in
letters and it looks like noise.

Colour is the same idea on a second channel: the amber version draws each light level in its own shade, so the
character and the colour agree. Four stills from the mono run, 1.25 s apart:
